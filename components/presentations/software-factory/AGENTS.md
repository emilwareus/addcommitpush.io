# The software factory 0.1

Presentation-specific instructions for this deck's slides, outline, notes and assets.

## Concrete examples are mandatory

- **Every substantive point, slide and step needs an EXTREMELY CONCRETE, visible example.** The audience must see exactly what Emil means.
- Use real screenshots, short GIFs, actual code, precise tool output, or a specific before-and-after comparison. Prefer examples from Emil's own projects and workflows.
- Show the actual instruction, changed code, failing check, workflow interaction or result being discussed. A generic diagram, abstract slogan or list of advice is not enough on its own.
- Design around the example first. Use only the few words needed to explain it. Keep screenshots readable; crop to the relevant part and highlight what matters. Split into more slides when needed.
- Do not invent successful runs, production output, measurements or customer facts. If an example is illustrative, label it clearly. If the real evidence is missing, record the exact asset needed and leave that point unfinished.
- **A slide is not complete until its example is included and visually checked.** Ensure it directly demonstrates the claim and is understandable at presentation size.

The cover remains title-only. The introduction uses actual project logos/images. There is no fixed slide-count limit; older 12-slide constraints are superseded. Keep the established addcommitpush.io profile and sparse text. GIFs are allowed when they demonstrate a concrete interaction or change, rather than decorate the slide.

## Small sub-headers are forbidden

Tiny uppercase kickers next to a title — "Working model", "Key idea", "In practice", "Remember", and anything like them — are AI slop. Do not add them. The title is the header. If a caveat matters, put it in the spoken notes or in the outline, not as a decorative mono label on the slide.
